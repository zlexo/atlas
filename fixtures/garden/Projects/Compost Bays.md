# Compost Bays

Three pallet bays behind the shed: filling, cooking, ready.

A heap works when it has about two parts brown material to one part green by
volume. Browns are dry leaves, straw, shredded cardboard and woody prunings;
they bring carbon and keep the heap open so air can reach the middle. Greens
are grass clippings, vegetable peelings, coffee grounds and fresh weeds before
they seed; they bring nitrogen and moisture and they are what heats the heap.

Too much green and the heap turns slimy and smells of ammonia, so add browns
and turn it. Too much brown and nothing happens for months, so add greens or a
bucket of fresh manure. The heap should feel like a wrung-out sponge when you
squeeze a handful.

Turn it every two weeks in summer and it is ready in three months. Leave it
alone and it still rots, it simply takes a year. Never add cooked food, meat,
diseased plants or perennial weed roots.
